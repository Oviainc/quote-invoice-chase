Collection rate: the share of invoiced money that has actually arrived — payments received divided by everything invoiced. The weekly review watches it rise; a rate that stalls while the aging buckets grow is the signal to chase harder, oldest first.

Days to pay: the average number of days between an invoice being created and being paid in full — the days-sales-outstanding reading of the invoices on record here. It counts only invoices with a payment date on record, so it never guesses. Watch it fall as reminders land earlier; a number that climbs while the collection rate stalls is the signal to shorten terms or chase sooner.

Payment reminder composer

Pick a saved invoice, choose a tone, and copy a ready-to-send reminder in the document language. Heads-up, polite, firm, and final templates are written — not machine-translated word for word. A rhythm that works: a heads-up a few days before the due date, a polite note just after it, a firm note the following week, and a final note after that. Every reminder we compose is logged on the invoice, so we always know what was sent and when. The documents list suggests the next step for every unpaid invoice — worked out from its due date and the reminders already logged — and picking an invoice here pre-selects that step, so what to send next is decided before the awkward moment arrives. Part-payments count too: record a payment on any invoice in the documents list and the balance due drops at once — the dashboard, the next-step suggestion, and every reminder then ask only for what is still owed, never for money already received. When a client owes more than one invoice, tick the statement box and we compose one message per client, not one per invoice: every unpaid invoice for that client, with its due date and balance, listed in a single statement with one total — and the reminder is logged on each invoice it covers. When it reads right, send it straight from here by email or WhatsApp, or copy it into any other channel. Every reminder and statement carries your saved payment details when you have added them, so the client never has to ask how to pay — the way to pay sits in the same message that asks for it. When a client gives a date, record it as a promise on the invoice in the documents list: the list shows the promised date, the next-step suggestion waits while that date is still ahead, and once a promised date passes unpaid the suggestion returns to chasing — a promise is tracked, not just heard. When late-payment terms are saved for the business, they print on every document and travel with every reminder and statement too, under a label in the document language — so the terms are stated where the client reads them, not remembered from an earlier conversation.
